    What you can do with this

    Choosing where you are referred

    If a GP needs to refer you for a physical or mental health condition, in most cases you have a legal right to choose the hospital or service, including many independent hospitals that treat NHS patients at no extra cost to the NHS. The shortlist is chosen by your GP, so tell them your preferences.

    Your choices in the NHS (nhs.uk)

    If you have waited a long time

    The NHS Choice Framework includes a right to ask to change provider when maximum waiting times are exceeded. Hospitals may also contact people who have waited a long time to ask if they would travel for earlier treatment.

    NHS Choice Framework (GOV.UK)

    What these numbers cannot tell you

    They describe everyone on a provider's list. Urgent and cancer referrals are seen sooner, and your own wait depends on your condition and your consultant. Use WaitLens to start a conversation with your GP or hospital, not to make clinical decisions.

    How WaitLens works

    Data

    Every month NHS England publishes a "Full CSV data file" of consultant-led referral-to-treatment (RTT) waits for every provider, commissioner and specialty in England, in one-week bands. A scheduled GitHub Actions job downloads the latest 25 months, adds the commissioner rows together for each provider, and publishes the results as static files. Locations come from the NHS Organisation Data Service and postcodes.io.

    Measures

    • Waiting now: pathways still waiting at the end of the month, the share waiting 18 weeks or less (the measure behind the 92% standard), the median wait so far, and the number waiting more than 52 weeks.
    • Recently treated: how long patients waited before treatment started, over the last three months combined so smaller services give steadier figures. "Admitted" treatment needs a hospital admission, such as an operation; "outpatient" treatment does not.
    • Medians and 92nd percentiles are estimated by linear interpolation inside the weekly band that contains them, the same approach NHS England describes. They are hidden when fewer than 20 pathways sit behind them.

    Checked against the published figures

    Each build recomputes national totals and compares them with NHS England's statistical press notices.

    Limitations

    • Figures are for providers, not hospital sites. Distances are to a provider's registered address, so large trusts with several hospitals may be closer or further than shown. Independent providers reported as one organisation with many sites have no distance.
    • Trusts that did not submit data for a month are missing for that month, as in NHS England's own provider tables.
    • Some patients are on more than one pathway, so pathways are not the same as people.
